
"The bees Knees" is a repository focused on solving a key problem with beekeeping.

This repository was developed in order to show adequate documentation of the development of my project for the purpose of scholarship technologies AS93601

Problem Statement:

Current beekeeping technology is designed for commercial use where honey extraction is centered. Most beginners do not understand the implications that come with using the commercial hive designs, this misalignment in expectations and technology causes three major problems.

Honey-bias:
The more commonly endorsed hives for beginners such as the Langstroth hive were designed in the 1800s and were centered around maximizing honey production.  Most new beekeepers do not fully understand the meaning behind beekeeping with their eyes set on the outcome, honey. Because the hive designs are centered around commercial use, beginners struggle with the concepts of how much honey to take without shifting the ecosystem of the colony. As a result, new beekeepers end up taking too much honey leaving the colony in a less sustainable situation going into winter. 

Hive’s viability:
Most beehive technology today is not built with beginners in mind as they are designed for larger operations. Due to this paradigm the hives do not have a Poka-Yoke design; it leaves beginners more susceptible to mistakes. These mistakes directly affect the colony's viability in a negative way.
 
Physical barriers:
In the world of beginner bee keeping hives like the Langstroth hive are commonly endorsed hives for beginners. These hives have physical barriers such as high time commitment and heavy hive boxes. Because of the architecture of these hives a beekeeper has to take off almost every component to access the brood box, the most important part of the hive. This can be physically demanding and take a lot of time which can be very impactful for the less physically able and/or people that have no time.

The Goal:

To develop a beehive that is centered around improving hive sustainability and user accessibility for new beekeeprs utilizing current day procedures and technology with evidence of improved functionality by the 28th of October 2026.

Specifications:

The design must be eccentrically designed with considerations of the bees over the user.
The final outcome should have a competitive development price to ensure it fills its gap effectively 
The final outcome should include all equipment associated with the hive and honey extraction (This excludes beekeeper suits, gloves, and any other equipment that a user would typically buy externally)
The design should be centered around sustainable material use and development 
The final design should effectively show consideration for both Nelson's heuristics and the relevant implications.
The final hive and extraction kit should be fully flat packed for easy shipping.
Any non-standardized process should have adequate reasoning behind their detachment from standardized methodology.
Must consider the user, their environment, and how they would interact with the outcome. 
 For this goal to be successful the outcome must be a considerable option for beginner beekeepers.
The outcome needs specific evidence of how it improves the hive sustainability and the user accessibility.

Relevant implications:

Accessibility
Is the beehive accessible for the end user in terms of cost, knowledge, and time?
Future proofing
Does the design consider past and future technology with considerations of how other technology will adapt around it.?
Ethical
Is it safe for both the people and the bees and is it a bee centric design?
End User
Is it built for beginners with considerations of mistakes and implications that come with inexperienced people?
Aesthetics
Does it blend into the environment effectively? The environment is broad so what design era will effectively accommodate for that.
Functionality
Does it still hold all of the core functionality of a traditional beehive effectively?
Usability
Are the common tasks for the bees and the beekeepers easy to do or are they inconvenient or lengthy?
