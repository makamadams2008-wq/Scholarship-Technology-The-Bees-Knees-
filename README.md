

"The Bee's Knees" is a repository focused on solving a key problem with beekeeping.

This repository was developed in order to show adequate documentation of the development of my project for the purpose of scholarship technologies AS93601.

Problem Statement:

Current beekeeping technology is designed for commercial use where honey extraction is centered. Most beginners do not understand the implications that come with using the commercial hive designs; this misalignment in expectations and technology causes three major problems.

There is a lack of technology surrounding beginner beekeepers to support them in a way that creates a balanced relationship between bees and humans. Because of this, beginners tend to put the hive in an unsatisfactory position when considering current-day technology. Three major problems put the hives in this position, which are time management and organization, the hive's viability, and honey bias.

Honey bias:

The more commonly endorsed hives for beginners, such as the Langstroth hive, were designed in the 1800s and were centered around maximizing honey production. Most new beekeepers do not fully understand the meaning behind beekeeping with their eyes set on the outcome, honey. Because the hive designs are centered around commercial use, beginners struggle with the concepts of how much honey to take without shifting the ecosystem of the colony. As a result, new beekeepers end up taking too much honey, leaving the colony in a less sustainable situation going into winter. 

Hive’s viability:

Most beehive technology today is not built with beginners in mind, as it is designed for larger operations. Due to this paradigm, the hives do not have a Poka-Yoke design; it leaves beginners more susceptible to mistakes. These mistakes directly affect the colony's viability in a negative way. Additionally, the hives are also developed to be used with specialized, expensive machinery that can cost a large sum of money for beginners with smaller operations.

 

Time management and organization:

In the world of beginner beekeeping, hives like the Langstroth hive are commonly endorsed hives for beginners. These hives have barriers such as high time commitment and high organization requirements. With many people today having full schedules, it can be hard to put the time aside to look after a hive correctly, especially when there are no reminders set in place, like a calendar or notification system.

Goal:

To develop an accessible beehive targeted at improving the symbiotic relationship between bees and humans by leveraging new regenerative technology. The final beehive should target the three main problems of time management and organization, the hive's viability, and honey bias.

Symbiosis Definition:

The interaction between two different organisms living in close physical association, typically to the advantage of both.

Target Audience:

New beekeepers who are looking at buying a first beehive with low skills or knowledge.

Specifications:

Regenerative Design:

- At least 90% of the materials by mass must be regenerative and sustainable.

- All structural components of the beehive must be fully recyclable or biodegradable.

- As a whole the hive must have a negative carbon footprint.

Symbiotic Relationship:

- When developing new technology, the health of the hive must be considered over the accessibility of the user.

- All beehive tasks should be achievable by the majority of complete beginners with less than 10 minutes of instruction and no longer than twice as long to execute when compared to the Langstroth hive.

- Any technology to do with the development of a beehive must support the bees in a passive manner, where the bees don't need to rely on the hive.

Hive viability:

- The beehive should consider the natural environment of bees and have at least three measurable improvements over the Langstroth hive that support the bee's viability with little to no negative impact, some exsmaples of this to look into are hive energy conseration, imporved varro mite midigation, or imporved hive humidity managment.

- All materials should be 100% bee safe.

- The beehive must have poka-yoke features so the user is less susceptible to making mistakes, such as flipping frames or crushing the queen, especially if the design is not as intuitive as the standards.

Accessible:

- The final beehive must have a total batch production price of no more than 500 NZD by incorporating only high-value features.

- The final beehive must include any proprietary technology in its overall package and budget.

- The final beehive must be flatpackable for cheaper shipping so more money can be put into more important development than expensive shipping.

- The final beehive should consider both the average user and the user range in its construction.

- All non-proprietary technology must have valid reasoning supported with evidence that demonstrates its strengths over standardized technology.

Time Management and Organization:

- The beehive must have integrated technology to promote the regular intervention of the user rather than trying to replace them.

- The beehive should have an app developed alongside it to help provide additional information for the user about their hive and general information.

-

Other:

- Any unsafe aspects of the hive must be clearly documented.

- Every new development should undergo evaluation to support its claims with concrete proof of overall improvement and consideration of drawbacks.

- The final hive must match the aesthetics of a range of expected environments effectively.

- When batch constructed, each hive must take less than 16 hours' worth of time to manufacture (this means one hive can take more than a day as long as it takes no more than 16 hours to produce).
